Chronic insomnia — difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early — affects roughly one in ten adults and significantly impairs mood, concentration, and quality of life. Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is the most effective treatment available, producing better long-term outcomes than sleep medication and without the risks of dependence or tolerance.

CBT-I is recommended as the first-line treatment for chronic insomnia by the American College of Physicians and the American Academy of Sleep Medicine.

## Who this is for

CBT-I is effective for adults with:

- Difficulty falling asleep at the start of the night
- Waking during the night and struggling to return to sleep
- Early morning awakening
- Chronic insomnia lasting more than three months
- Insomnia persisting even after addressing contributing medical factors

CBT-I works well alongside medication and is often the most effective path for people who want to reduce or stop using sleep aids. It is effective for primary insomnia as well as insomnia co-occurring with anxiety, depression, chronic pain, or other conditions.

## How it works

CBT-I addresses the behavioral patterns and beliefs that perpetuate insomnia over time — patterns that often outlast the original trigger (a stressful event, illness, life change) and take on a life of their own. Treatment typically includes:

- **Sleep restriction:** consolidating sleep to strengthen the drive to sleep and re-establish the association between bed and sleep
- **Stimulus control:** rebuilding the bed and bedroom as cues for sleep rather than wakefulness or worry
- **Cognitive restructuring:** addressing unhelpful beliefs about sleep that maintain the cycle of insomnia
- **Sleep hygiene:** tailored adjustments to sleep environment and pre-sleep routine

CBT-I typically involves 6–8 sessions. Most clients see significant improvement within the first few weeks of treatment.
