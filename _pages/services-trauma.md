Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and trauma-related concerns respond well to treatment. Research on evidence-based trauma therapies is among the strongest in clinical psychology — several approaches produce meaningful, lasting recovery in the majority of people who complete them.

## Who this is for

I work with children, adolescents, and adults who have experienced traumatic events including abuse, neglect, accidents, medical trauma, loss, and other adverse experiences. Trauma symptoms can include intrusive memories or nightmares, avoidance of reminders, negative changes in mood and thinking, and heightened arousal or reactivity. You do not need a formal PTSD diagnosis to benefit from trauma-focused treatment.

## How it works

I use evidence-based approaches including trauma-focused cognitive-behavioral therapy (TF-CBT) and cognitive processing therapy (CPT), tailored to each client's history, goals, and pace. These treatments help clients process traumatic memories in a structured, supported way — reducing their hold on day-to-day functioning — and address patterns of thought and avoidance that maintain symptoms over time.

For younger children, I often work primarily or jointly with parents, drawing on approaches with strong evidence for this age group.

A focused course of trauma treatment typically spans 12–20 sessions, though pace and length are always calibrated to your specific needs. Some clients complete treatment in fewer sessions; others benefit from longer work, particularly with complex or developmental trauma histories.

## My background

Before clinical practice, I spent several years as a clinical scientist studying the neuroscience of trauma and adversity — specifically, how early experiences of threat shape the developing brain and confer risk for PTSD and related conditions. I bring that research background to clinical work: the treatments I use are ones I have studied from both inside and outside the therapy room.
