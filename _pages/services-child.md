Disruptive behavior in children — defiance, aggression, difficulty following rules, frequent outbursts — is one of the most common reasons families seek mental health support. Evidence-based behavioral approaches are highly effective for these concerns, and research consistently shows that working with parents is often the most powerful lever available.

## Who this is for

I work with families of children and adolescents experiencing:

- Oppositional defiant disorder (ODD) and conduct problems
- ADHD-related behavioral challenges
- Frequent tantrums, defiance, or difficulty following rules
- Aggression or explosive behavior at home or school
- Difficulty with transitions, compliance, or peer relationships

Especially for younger children, the most effective approach is often to work primarily — or entirely — with parents rather than the child directly. This is not because the child's experience does not matter, but because parents are the most powerful agents of change in a young child's environment. Helping parents respond consistently and skillfully produces faster, more lasting results than individual child therapy alone in many cases.

## How it works

Treatment draws on behavioral parent training approaches with strong research support. Sessions focus on helping parents understand the principles driving their child's behavior, develop effective and consistent responses, and build a stronger relationship alongside clearer expectations.

For older children and adolescents, I may work jointly with both the child and parent. For younger children, I typically meet with parents alone or with brief involvement from the child.

A focused course of treatment typically spans 8–16 sessions, depending on the child's age and the complexity of concerns.
